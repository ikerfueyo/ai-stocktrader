# Going live with Alpaca

`AlpacaBroker` (`lib/server/broker/alpaca/`) implements the same `Broker` interface as the simulator, and `BROKER=alpaca` with the Broker API's OAuth client credentials selects it. The adapter is unit-tested against mocked HTTP, the shared scenario suite has run against Alpaca's Broker API sandbox (results below) as well as `SimBroker`, and on 2026-11-10 the app itself ran on the sandbox, from onboarding to trades (below). With `BROKER=alpaca` the app reads accounts only through `Broker`; the last section lists what that path still lacks.

This document lists what's left before real accounts and real money. Most of it isn't code. The code that's left is in its own section at the end.

## Before anything else: check the connection, then run the scenarios in the sandbox

`npm run check:alpaca` checks what the app needs to connect, changing nothing at Alpaca: the readiness list (`lib/server/readiness.ts`), an OAuth token, the clock, the calendar, the accounts, and each event stream opened from 10 minutes back (`CHECK_SINCE_MINUTES` to look further). The server runs the same readiness check when it starts: with the sandbox its problems are warnings; with `ALPACA_BROKER_ENV=production` it refuses to start while any is left: placeholder agreements (`AGREEMENT_DOCUMENTS`), `REQUIRE_MFA` off, no `ALPACA_ACH_DAILY_LIMIT`, Plaid's sandbox with Plaid's keys, a test-only switch (`PRICE_OVERRIDES`, `MARKET_HOURS_OVERRIDE`, `BROKER_LEDGER`, `SIM_HTB_SYMBOLS`, `BACKGROUND_JOBS=off`), or a development build. Like the scenarios, it must run outside Claude Code's Bash sandbox (TLS, below).

The app on the sandbox: the `next-dev-alpaca` launch configuration (`.claude/launch.json`) runs `next dev` with `BROKER=alpaca`, `ALPACA_BROKER_ENV=sandbox` and `REQUIRE_MFA=false`. Only one server runs the background jobs (the leader's lock), so stop the simulator's server first.

1. Get Broker API sandbox credentials from Alpaca's Broker dashboard. These are an OAuth client ID and secret, and sandbox and production credentials aren't interchangeable.
2. Put them in `.env.local` as `ALPACA_BROKER_CLIENT_ID` and `ALPACA_BROKER_CLIENT_SECRET`, with `ALPACA_BROKER_ENV=sandbox` (the default).
3. Run `npm run test:scenarios:sandbox` while the market is open. It refuses production credentials. Each run opens new sandbox accounts with fake identities. It moves only sandbox money.
4. Set `SCENARIO_PATIENCE=180` (15 minutes at the default 5s wait). The sandbox's transfer worker runs every 10 minutes (at :00, :10, :20 and so on), so the default 5 minutes misses about half of the deposits' completions. Alpaca support (2026-11-10) says a transfer created after its bank link is APPROVED, as `fund` in the scenarios waits for, is usually picked up within seconds, and funds count toward buying power from SENT_TO_CLEARING, before COMPLETE (the app run that day saw cash and buying power only at COMPLETE; below); the 19 deposits checked so far all completed at a 10-minute mark, up to 9 minutes after they were created. A full run then takes well over an hour; `SCENARIO=<part of a name>` runs one scenario, and several can run at once as separate processes, each opening its own accounts.
5. Set `SCENARIO_ACCOUNT_ID` to an existing account's ID (not its number) for what a new account can't do: the short and close position scenarios need margin with shorting, which support enables per account, and the withdraw and wire scenarios need cash past the 7 business days' deposit hold. The account must be ACTIVE, flat in the two symbols with no open orders in them, and have at least $3,000 withdrawable; each run withdraws $1,515 from it. Without it, withdraw and wire are skipped, and short and close position open new accounts and wait for shorting. The close scenario always needs a new account whose deposit can be withdrawn, so it's skipped in the sandbox. A new deposit-hold scenario checks the hold itself on a new account: `cash_withdrawable` 0, `cash_transferable` the deposit, and a withdrawal refused with 403 40310000.

The eleventh, the ledger scenario, now allows for fees taken from cash before their FEE activity posts (below). What the run showed:
- **Withdrawals.** `cash_withdrawable` stays 0 after an ACH deposit completes, and an outgoing ACH transfer or wire is then refused with `forbidden.` (403, 40310000). It's a deposit hold, not a correspondent setting. Alpaca support said on 2026-11-06 that deposits generally become withdrawable on the 7th business day, and the sandbox does the same, counting business days after the deposit's day:
  - Both deposits completed on 2026-10-28 (407381006, 407700444) were withdrawable at 4:59 p.m. ET on 2026-11-06, their 7th business day.
  - The 17 completed on 2026-10-29 still showed 0 then, though 8 calendar days had passed and their `expires_at` (2026-11-05, 7 calendar days on) too, so that field isn't the release date. All 17 were withdrawable at 4:51 p.m. ET on 2026-11-09, their 7th business day.

  Alpaca support confirmed on 2026-11-10 that production holds ACH deposits for 7 business days too, with no fixed release time of day, so the app should read `cash_withdrawable` rather than work out the date (the Broker path already does). `cash_withdrawable` is what can leave to an external bank by ACH or wire, net of held deposits, pending outgoing transfers and other restrictions; `cash_transferable` is what's available for internal movements such as journals (JNLC), held deposits included, which is why it showed the full amount during the hold. In production, ACH deposits post once the bank confirms the file was processed, usually in the afternoon or evening ET. A withdrawal from a released account hasn't been tried. The simulator now holds a completed ACH deposit until 9:00 ET on the 7th banking day after it completes (`depositReleaseAt` in `lib/server/broker/sim/funding.ts`, `SIM_DEPOSIT_HOLD_DAYS`), keeps it out of `cash_withdrawable` but not `cash_transferable` or buying power, and refuses a withdrawal beyond `cash_withdrawable` with Alpaca's 403 40310000. It counts Federal Reserve banking days, so Veterans Day and Columbus Day don't count; whether Alpaca's business days skip them is unchecked. The Withdraw page now caps the amount at `cash_withdrawable` and says the rest is held: open orders, unsettled sales, and otherwise deposits the broker still holds.
- **Fees.** Alpaca takes regulatory fees from cash at the trade and posts the FEE activity later. Three fills on 2026-10-29 left cash $0.04 below the fills' net, exactly the simulator's SEC fee ($0.0288) and CAT fee ($0.000017), each rounded up to the cent. Until the FEE activity posts, the activity ledger and Alpaca's `cash` differ by the day's fees; the simulator deducts them only at the end of the day.
- **Lag.** Right after a fill or a cancel, the activities and the open-orders list can be behind. A fill was missing from the activities (6 shares in the replayed lots against 9 in the position), and a cancelled order was still listed as open, though `getOrder` already said `canceled`. Neither recurred on a rerun.
- **Sandbox and TLS.** Inside Claude Code's Bash sandbox, Node can't read the macOS certificate trust settings, so every call to Alpaca fails with `fetch failed`. Run the suite outside it.

## Not code

### The broker partnership
- **The agreement and Alpaca's review.** That means a Broker API agreement with Alpaca, as a fully disclosed introducing relationship where each user has their own Alpaca account, and Alpaca's review of the app, its flows and its marketing before production credentials are issued.
- **KYC and CIP move to Alpaca.** Alpaca reviews applications. The simulator's review rules (`lib/server/broker/sim/kyc.ts`) only stood in for that. The app keeps validating the form (`lib/kyc.ts`).
- **Real agreements and Form CRS links.** The `/agreements/[name]` pages are placeholders. Each one has to be replaced with the real document or Alpaca's link:
  - the customer agreement, the account agreement and the margin agreement;
  - the IRA disclosure statement and custodial agreement;
  - the app's own Form CRS and privacy notice, if counsel decides the app needs them.

  Each goes in `AGREEMENT_DOCUMENTS` (JSON: `{ "customer_agreement": { "url": "https://…", "revision": "19.2026.10" }, … }`, for `customer_agreement`, `account_agreement`, `margin_agreement`, `ira_disclosure`, `form_crs` and `privacy_notice`; `lib/server/agreements.ts`). The forms then link to the real document with its revision, `/agreements/[name]` redirects to it, and the server checks that the revision the page showed is still the current one (409 otherwise), sends it as the agreement's `revision` to Alpaca and keeps it in `account_attestations.details.revision` for Form CRS, the privacy notice and the IRA disclosure. Unset, each is the placeholder with revision `placeholder`, which isn't sent (Alpaca then records its current one). Get Alpaca's links and revision numbers for its agreements.
- **Fees and limits.** Alpaca's fee schedule (revised Oct 12, 2026) confirmed the simulator's figures: the $15 outgoing domestic wire, the $25 ACH return, the 6.50% margin rate, the regulatory fees (SEC, TAF and CAT) and how the HTB borrow fee is computed. Check it again before launch, since it changes without notice. Outgoing ACATS ($25) and international wires ($35) aren't offered. The vendored Broker spec now has incoming ACATS (`/v1beta1/acats`: full and partial transfers of U.S. equities and cash, with contra brokers, statuses and assets), which the app doesn't model for any account. IRA transfers and rollovers aren't offered at all ("not yet supported", per Alpaca's IRA overview, Oct 2026). Confirm the production ACH limits and cutoff times. With `BROKER=alpaca` the forms show `ALPACA_WIRE_FEE` (default $15), `ALPACA_ACH_RETURN_FEE` ($25) and `ALPACA_ACH_DAILY_LIMIT` (50,000, a guess until Alpaca confirms it; `lib/server/funding.ts`), and the withdrawal check adds that wire fee.

### Security
- **SOC 2.** Alpaca expects partners to pass a security assessment.
- **Multi-factor sign-in.** Done in code: with `REQUIRE_MFA` (on by default with `BROKER=alpaca`), orders, transfers and the sensitive changes below answer 403 until the user turns on two-step verification (Clerk's `twoFactorEnabled`; `lib/server/sensitive.ts`). Still to do: allow an authenticator app (and backup codes) in the production Clerk instance, and turn on "Require multi-factor authentication" there so new users set it up at sign-in (the sign-in page shows Clerk's `TaskSetupMFA` for that task; untried until it's on).
- **Re-authentication.** Done in code: withdrawals, bank links and wire recipients (adding and removing), the account's details and configurations, the W-9, opening an IRA and closing the account need a factor verified in the last 10 minutes (Clerk's "strict" reverification). The route answers 403 with Clerk's hint and the page's `useReverifiedRequest` opens Clerk's dialog and retries. Try the dialog once against the production Clerk instance.
- **Reg S-P.** Write an incident response plan for Reg S-P, including the customer notices required by the 2024 amendments.
- **Secrets.** Store the OAuth client secret and the Plaid secret in a secrets manager, not in `.env.local`. Rotate them on a schedule.
- **Tax IDs.** An application's full SSN goes from the form straight to Alpaca and is never stored (accounts come back masked: `redactAccount`). Opening an IRA asks for it again. An account update can't carry a tax ID or date of birth (400 before anything reaches the broker), and a tax ID that Alpaca's error messages echo back is masked to its last 4 digits before it's logged or shown (`redactTaxIds` in `lib/server/broker/alpaca/http.ts`). Keep it out of analytics and error reports, including any request logging added later.
- **Penetration test.** Run one before launch.

### What the simulator can't model
Alpaca does these itself. The simulator's versions were approximations and go away with `BROKER=alpaca`:
- real fills, routing and liquidity, halts and LULD pauses, and trade busts and corrections;
- hard-to-borrow availability, borrow rates and locates (the Broker API has none);
- house margin requirements per symbol and the actual margin interest rate;
- voluntary corporate actions and their elections;
- real bank verification and ACH returns;
- real statements, confirmations and tax forms;
- OCC's assignment process;
- the order in which a margin liquidation closes positions. The simulator closes first what releases the most requirement per dollar. The sandbox can't force a margin call, so ask Alpaca how it orders a liquidation.
