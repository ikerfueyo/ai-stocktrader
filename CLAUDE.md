# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Overview

AI StockTrader is a mock-trading web app for U.S. common stocks (no ETFs, funds or OTC listings): a single Next.js 16 (App Router) app in TypeScript, with pages and API route handlers in one project, backed by PostgreSQL, with sign-in through Clerk; each user has their own account. Stock prices and news come from Alpha Vantage; buy/sell rankings and per-ticker analysis come from OpenAI (`gpt-6-luna`, reasoning off): the rankings from news sentiment, and each ticker's structured analysis from its news plus computed technicals, its earnings against estimates, estimate revisions and next report date, and its valuation and analyst consensus. The prompts ask for the likely price effect over the horizon (results against expectations, what's already priced in), not the tone of the news. A ticker only appears in a ranking when its own analysis agrees. Every fresh verdict is logged (`verdict_log`) and scored later against SPY (`GET /api/analysis/track-record`).

It's being turned into a simulated brokerage that follows Alpaca's Broker API and U.S. rules closely enough to switch to the real Alpaca later (phases in `docs/brokerage-roadmap.md`). Phase 0 (done) added the Broker types and interface (`lib/server/broker/`), the market-data layer with Alpaca's free data, the stock universe, extended-hours sessions and the job runner. Phase 1 (done) added brokerage accounts: onboarding with KYC review in the simulator (`SimBroker`), the account gate, Settings and the account switcher. Phase 2 (done) added funding: bank links, ACH and wire transfers with settlement, returns and fees in the simulator, and the Transfers, Deposit and Withdraw pages. Phase 3 (done) moved the account's ledger into the simulator: fills are FILL activities that close and open FIFO tax lots (the positions' only source), settle T+1 and accrue regulatory fees deducted at the end of the day; splits and dividends post per account; the trading account and positions follow Alpaca's shapes. Phase 4 (done) moved orders into the simulator: Alpaca's order object, its validation rules (tick sizes, fractional and notional limits, extended hours, auctions, no order through zero, potential wash trades), every order type and time in force, bracket, OCO and OTO legs, partial fills, replace and cancel, matched by the simulator's own job. Phase 5 (done) replaced the app's 150%/130% short-collateral model with Alpaca's margin: buying power at 1x, 2x overnight and 4x intraday, Reg T initial and house maintenance margin, margin calls issued after the close and liquidated at their deadline (or at once when equity is far below the requirement), margin interest, and shorting only with a margin account and $2,000 of equity, hard-to-borrow stocks needing a locate and paying a borrow fee. Phase 6 (done) added the corporate actions beyond splits and cash dividends (stock dividends, cash, stock and mixed mergers, spinoffs, symbol changes and worthless removals, from Alpaca when keys are set), applied to each account's lots with Alpaca's activity types, and their effect on open orders as Alpaca handles it. Phase 7 (done) added account documents in Alpaca's shape (daily trade confirmations and their JSON, monthly statements, the account application and the consolidated 1099 with FIFO lots, wash sales, 1099-DIV and 1099-INT) as printable pages, backup withholding while the W-9 calls for it, and a gains and losses report. Phase 8 (done) added the app's own automation, which works on any broker: notifications from the broker's event streams (a bell in the header and a notifications center with preferences per kind, in the app and in the browser), recurring buys, dividend reinvestment and price alerts. Phase 9 (done) added options at Alpaca's levels 1 to 3: the chains (Alpha Vantage's closing chain with each quote modeled now from the stock's price, as the data plan lacks realtime options), single-contract and mleg orders, the universal spread rule's requirement and options buying power, covered calls locking their shares, exercise, do-not-exercise, automatic exercise, assignment and expiration, early assignment before ex-dates, option fees, and options on the 1099-B. Phase 10 (done) added IRAs (traditional and Roth, Alpaca's `account_type` ira): opened from the individual account's reviewed details with beneficiaries, at 1x without shorting and with options up to level 2, contributions per tax year within the IRS limit across the user's IRAs, distributions with a reason and federal and state withholding, Forms 1099-R and 5498 instead of the consolidated 1099, and no backup withholding. Phase 11 (done) added `AlpacaBroker` (`lib/server/broker/alpaca/`), the same `Broker` interface over Alpaca's Broker API (OAuth client credentials, REST, the SSE event streams, Plaid processor tokens for bank links), not yet connected to a real account, and a scenario suite written against `Broker` that runs on the simulator (and later Alpaca's sandbox); `docs/going-live.md` lists what's left before going live. The app reads the active account's ledger (the account switcher's, else the newest open one); background jobs go account by account.

## Architecture

**Server** (route handlers in `app/api/**/route.ts`, all paths under `/api` because the pages use `/trading/[ticker]`, `/portfolio` and `/transactions`; logic in `lib/server/`, each module marked `server-only`):
- `lib/server/errors.ts`: `HttpError`, `UpstreamError`, `errorResponse` (the error contract below) and `withUser`, which wraps every handler: it reads the Clerk session with `auth()` (made available by `clerkMiddleware()` in `proxy.ts`, Next 16's name for middleware), answers 401 without a signed-in user, passes the user ID to the handler (every account query and `withAccountLock` take it), and turns thrown errors into error responses. So every route, including `/api/rankings` and `/api/analysis/[ticker]`, needs a signed-in user. Requests are same-origin, so the Clerk session cookie authenticates them; there's no CORS.
- `lib/server/broker/`: the brokerage the app will trade through. `types.ts` names Alpaca's Broker API objects (`Account`, `TradeAccount`, `Order`, `Position`, `Activity`, `Transfer`, `ACHRelationship`, `Asset`, `AccountDocument`, ...) from the generated types, snake_case with decimals as strings exactly as Alpaca returns them; `broker.ts` is the `Broker` interface, one method per Broker API endpoint, which the simulator (`SimBroker`) and `AlpacaBroker` implement, and `brokerMode()` (`BROKER`: `sim` or `alpaca`; `getBroker()` makes the one it names); `errors.ts` is `BrokerError` (Alpaca's `{ code, message }` with an HTTP status, an `HttpError`); `conformance.ts` validates objects against the vendored specs with Ajv (tests only, reads the files). `HttpError` itself is in `lib/server/httpError.ts` (not `server-only`, so pure modules can throw it); `errors.ts` re-exports it, and `errorResponse` recognizes it and its subclasses with `isHttpError` (its `isHttpError` marker as well as `instanceof`, since a module loaded twice has two copies of the class).
- The simulator (`lib/server/broker/sim/`, `getBroker()` in `lib/server/broker/index.ts` returns its `SimBroker`, created per module rather than with `singleton`: it keeps no state, and a `globalThis` instance outlived hot reloads and threw errors from stale classes): `accounts.ts` runs Alpaca's account endpoints on `sim_accounts` (create, get, update, close, document upload, configurations, options approval) and `emitEvent`; `kyc.ts` (pure) decides reviews (`decideApplication`: last name `Reject`, `ActionRequired` or `Review` picks the outcome for testing, and a control-person, affiliated or politically exposed disclosure always goes to manual review), steps them (`nextReviewStep`: SUBMITTED, then APPROVED and ACTIVE, or APPROVAL_PENDING, ACTION_REQUIRED (to review once a document is uploaded) or REJECTED; ACCOUNT_UPDATED back to ACTIVE), says which profile changes are reviewed (`updateNeedsReview`: address, name, citizenship, employer, a disclosure turning true), sets default configurations (`defaultConfigurations`: 2x margin and shorting only with the margin agreement) and checks changes (`configurationError`), and decides options approvals (`decideOptionsApproval`: a lower level without the experience); `shapes.ts` (pure) turns rows into Alpaca's objects (`toAccount`, `toAsset` with a name-based UUID when Alpaca gave none, `toCalendar`, `toClock`, `toOptionsApproval`). Reviews advance when due, from the `sim-accounts` job and whenever an account is read. `subscribe` polls `sim_events` every 2s. The Broker methods of later phases throw `BrokerError` 501. The application rules themselves are in `lib/kyc.ts` (shared with the onboarding form): `validateApplication` (U.S. tax residents 18 or older, citizens or permanent residents, at a residential U.S. address, a valid SSN (no 000, 666 or 9xx area) or ITIN, the disclosures' details when one is true, the customer and account agreements, the W-9 certification, Form CRS and the privacy notice delivered) and `maskTaxId`.

Routes. Market data (prices, rankings, analyses) and its caches are shared by all users:
- `GET /api/analysis/[ticker]`: `analyzeTicker` fetches the ticker's latest news (dots become hyphens, since the news endpoint expects `BRK-A` where prices use `BRK.A`; articles below `MIN_NEWS_RELEVANCE` to the ticker are dropped) plus technicals computed from its full daily closes with its splits undone (`adjustForSplits`, from `loadCorporateActions`, so a split isn't read as a crash) (`computeTechnicals` in `lib/server/technicals.ts`: 5-session, 1-month and 3-month returns, the 1- and 3-month returns less SPY's (from SPY's compact series, shared with the performance chart), distance from the 50- and 200-day averages, 14-day RSI), its earnings and estimates (`summarizeEarnings`), and its company overview (valuation, analyst target price and rating counts), each left out when Alpha Vantage has none. The system prompt and `ANALYSIS_RULES` frame the task as the likely price effect over the horizon: whether the news is new or already reflected in the recent returns, results against expectations over tone, and an earnings report within the horizon as a catalyst and a risk. It returns `{ verdict, analysis, horizon, evidence, counterarguments, risks, catalysts, sources, generatedAt, dataAsOf, model }`: `BUY` (the evidence points to the price rising over the fixed 1-to-4-week horizon), `SELL` (falling; selling includes shorting) or `HOLD` (only when balanced or too thin); `analysis` is the thesis, one paragraph that must agree with the verdict; each list has 2 to 4 items; the model cites articles by number, and the server keeps only real ones as `sources` (title, link, time) and strips inline numbers from the text. No confidence percentages or price targets (JSON mode, temperature 0). Article numbers left in the text, in brackets or parentheses, are stripped. Cached per ticker for 30 minutes with its inputs (`publicAnalysis` leaves them out of the response). Each fresh verdict is inserted into `verdict_log` in the background (a failure is only logged). `peekTickerVerdict` reads a finished cached verdict without fetching.
- `GET /api/rankings`: `{ buy: [...], sell: [...] }`, up to 5 tickers each (`buildRankings`, cached 30 minutes). It fetches the latest 1000 market news articles, groups them by the companies each is mainly about (`mainTickers`: the most relevant tickers, so a bank's analyst note on another company isn't counted for the bank; crypto, forex, preferred, warrant, unit and rights symbols are skipped), and scores each stock from Alpha Vantage's per-ticker sentiment (sum / √articles). The 30 most bullish and 30 most bearish stocks (average sentiment at least ±0.15) go to one OpenAI call with their newest 4 articles each, and it shortlists up to 15 per side. Picking by coverage alone left the sell side nearly empty, since the most-covered stocks are mostly neutral or bullish. `confirmRankings` then runs `analyzeTicker` on the candidates in order and keeps only those whose verdict matches the side (`BUY` or `SELL`), so a side can have fewer than 5. Each refresh logs the funnel (articles, stocks, each candidate's verdict) to the server console. The listed tickers' cached analyses are kept at least as long as the rankings, so the Trading page shows the same verdict that put the stock in the ranking.

One verdict per prompt version, ticker and session (the last), only those at least 7 days old; splits are undone (`loadCorporateActions`, which fetches any ticker never fetched). - `GET /api/portfolio`: open positions `[{ ticker, quantity, avgCost, price, bid, ask, error }]`, from the account's open FIFO lots (`avgCost` is their average; splits are applied to the lots). Realized P&L is booked by each fill against the lots it closes. `computePositions` (average cost) still replays the fills with their splits for the equity history and dividend eligibility. Rows include `previousClose`, for the Today column. `quantity` is signed: Trading-page sells past zero are intentional shorts, so a negative position is a short. Cost basis is average cost: adding to a position re-averages it, reducing it keeps the average, and a trade that flips the side opens the remainder at the trade price. Quantities are rounded to 6 decimals after each trade (`roundShares`), so float drift can't leave a tiny position open. Rows also carry `bid`/`ask`. Prices come from `fetchQuotes` (`lib/server/alphaVantage.ts`), with `Promise.allSettled`, so a ticker whose price fails gets `price: null` and an `error` instead of failing the whole response. `fetchQuotes` fetches every uncached ticker together with the premium bulk endpoints (`REALTIME_BULK_QUOTES` plus `REALTIME_BULK_BID_ASK_PRICES`, 100 symbols per call); a plan without them gets artificial sample data with a "premium endpoint" `message`, which `fetchAlphaVantage` turns into a `NotEntitledError`, and the endpoint is then skipped for an hour in favor of each ticker's compact `TIME_SERIES_DAILY` (same entitlement and newest-bar close as `/api/trading/[ticker]`, previous bar for the previous close). The current key's plan includes them. Without quoted bid/ask, a simulated spread of `SIMULATED_SPREAD_BPS` basis points (at least a cent, at most half the price so the bid stays positive; 0 turns it off and fills at the last price) is centered on the last price. Each ticker's quote is cached for 15s (failures aren't kept), so `/api/portfolio`, `/api/account` and trades share one call. The last price is remembered in memory, which `fetchPrices` falls back to (listing the ticker in `staleTickers`) when a fetch fails. `PRICE_OVERRIDES` (env JSON such as `{"TSTA": 120}`) fixes prices without Alpha Vantage, with no spread, for local testing only (the scenarios move them, with a volume, through `overridePrice`, refused in production).
- `GET /api/account` (`Account` in `lib/types.ts`): the broker's margin snapshot (`totalCash`, `buyingPower`, `regtBuyingPower`, `nonMarginableBuyingPower`, `reservedCash` (buying power held for open orders), `multiplier` and `maxMultiplier`, `equity`, `longMarketValue`, `shortMarketValue`, `initialMargin`, `maintenanceMargin`, `excessLiquidity`, `sma`, `debitBalance`, `marginRate`, `accruedInterest`, `marginStatus` (`ok`, `margin_call`, `liquidation`), `marginCalls`, `shortingEnabled` and `shortingBlockedReason`), with `unrealizedPnl` and `missingPrices` (`valueAccount` at the snapshot's prices), `realizedPnl`, `netDividends`, `positions`, `liquidations` (the past week's LIQUIDATION fills), `pendingTransferOut`, `withdrawableCash`, `unsettledProceeds` and `accruedFees`. Updates the account first (`updateAccount`), so it can write.
  - Total cash is a plain ledger: every activity's net amount (fills, deposits as they're credited, withdrawals when they leave, fees, dividends, interest), less queued withdrawals (`pendingTransferOut`), which are held from the moment they're requested. A short sale's proceeds land in it and a cover pays from it; on margin it goes negative (the debit).
  - Dividends (`postDividends`, from `updateAccount`): `planDividends` finds each stored dividend whose payment date has come, on a ticker held at its ex-date's open (`positionsAt`: trades before that day, splits through it), not yet posted, and the broker posts it as a DIV activity dated at the payment date's close. Longs receive shares × amount, shorts pay it.
  - `equity = total cash + Σ long market value - Σ short market value` (also net deposits + realized + unrealized P&L + net dividends - fees and interest; the tests check it), and `unrealizedPnl = Σ long (market value - cost basis) + Σ short (sale proceeds - market value)`, with cost basis and proceeds at `avgCost × shares`. Trades at the market price never change equity; the tests check this.
  - A position without a current price is valued at its last known price, or at its cost, and listed in `missingPrices`. Stale prices never approve a risky action or trigger a liquidation. During a scheduled session (the NYSE calendar), a quote that's end-of-day or more than 15 minutes old (`isStaleQuote`) counts as stale too: `fetchPrices` lists it in `staleTickers` (it's still shown). `recordFill` refuses a fill without a positive price and quantity.
  - Each user's account changes run one at a time through `withAccountLock(userId, fn)`: an in-process queue per user in front of a Postgres advisory lock (`pg_advisory_lock(hashtext(user_id))`) on a dedicated pool client, which `fn` gets. The broker's own changes take its account lock as well (`lockAccount`). The client is held for the whole call, Alpha Vantage requests included, so the pool allows 20 connections.
- `GET /api/assets/[symbol]`: the broker's Asset (marginable, shortable, `borrow_status`, margin requirements, fractionable), for the order ticket. `GET /api/locates/quotes?symbols=`, `GET /api/locates?status=&symbol=` and `POST /api/locates` (Alpaca's CreateLocateRequest, on the active account): locates for hard-to-borrow short sales.
- Funding: `GET`/`POST /api/accounts/[id]/ach` (bank links; Alpaca's CreateACHRelationshipRequest, or `{ plaid: { public_token, account_id } }` from Plaid Link, made into a processor token (400 without Plaid); 409 with another active link), `POST /api/plaid/link-token` (`{ linkToken, expiration }`; 404 without Plaid) and `DELETE .../ach/[relationshipId]` (422 with transfers in progress); `GET`/`POST /api/accounts/[id]/banks` (`{ name, bank_code, account_number }`, ABA only) and `DELETE .../banks/[bankId]`; `GET /api/accounts/[id]/transfers` (Alpaca's Transfers, newest first; `expires_at` is when the money is expected) and `POST` (`{ transfer: { type: ach | wire, direction, amount, relationshipId | bankId } }`; the active account only (409 otherwise), checked with `requireAccount`; a withdrawal is checked against the broker's margin snapshot (`withdrawalError`: not during a call, no more than `withdrawable`) inside the account lock, for the amount plus any wire fee; 201); `DELETE .../transfers/[transferId]` (cancel while QUEUED); `GET /api/funding` (`FundingTerms`: minimum, daily ACH limit, instant limit, wire and return fees, `plaid` (Alpaca with Plaid's keys) and `simulated`). IDs that aren't UUIDs get 404 (or 422 in a body) before any query.
- `GET /api/transfers`: the user's cash ledger, newest first (`loadCashActivity`: the broker's CSD, CSW, FEE, DIV, INT and WH activities as DEPOSIT, WITHDRAW, FEE, DIVIDEND_IN/OUT, INTEREST and WITHHOLDING rows, and the cash of MA and CIL activities as CORPORATE_ACTION_IN/OUT; the history counts withholding as money leaving, like a withdrawal); `POST` is gone (405). The performance history reads the same ledger: deposits and withdrawals are external flows, fees count against the return.
- `GET /api/portfolio/[ticker]/analysis`: the same shape for an open position (404 if there's none). `analyzePosition` derives the verdict from the ticker's `analyzeTicker` verdict, so it can't contradict the Trading page: a long is `SELL` on `SELL` and `HOLD` otherwise, a short is `COVER` on `BUY` and `HOLD` otherwise. A second OpenAI call (temperature 0) then writes the structured analysis for the position explaining that verdict, from the same inputs `analyzeTicker` used (cached with it, so no extra Alpha Vantage call) plus the position: shares, average cost, unrealized P&L and weight in the account's equity (the route values the account with the cached quotes). Cached 30 minutes per user, ticker, side and verdict.

All AI prompts live on the server (`lib/server/analysis.ts`). There's deliberately no generic prompt endpoint, so the OpenAI key can't be used as an open proxy. Shared helpers: `cachePromise` (`lib/server/cache.ts`; caches a promise per key, shared by concurrent callers; failures aren't kept), `fetchAlphaVantage` (starts requests at least 300ms apart and at most `ALPHA_VANTAGE_REQUESTS_PER_MINUTE` in any 61 seconds across all routes, queuing the rest, because Alpha Vantage rejects more than 5 a second with a "Burst pattern detected" `Information` reply and more than the plan's per-minute limit, which can still trip during a long run of calls, so it's retried up to twice after a pause), `fetchNews` (`NEWS_SENTIMENT` sorted latest first, as `{ title, summary, published, tickers: [{ ticker, relevance }] }`) and `fetchTickerNews` (hyphenated symbol, 404 when no relevant article), `askAnalyst` (system prompt plus OpenAI call) and `analyzeNews` (articles with their publication times, then the prompt), and `errorResponse`.

Error contract: `withUser` catches every handler's errors and calls `errorResponse`, which logs `METHOD path: message` and always returns `{ error: message }` with one of these statuses:
- 401: no signed-in user (`withUser`: no session, or an invalid or expired one).
- 502: a bad reply from Alpha Vantage, OpenAI, or the model. Alpha Vantage reports rate limits, missing entitlements and bad symbols with HTTP 200 and a `Note`/`Information`/`Error Message` body (or `{}`), so `fetchAlphaVantage` throws an `UpstreamError` whenever the expected key is missing. OpenAI SDK errors also map to 502.
- 400: a rejected request, such as a withdrawal without enough withdrawable cash, or one during a margin call, or a locate that isn't a round lot (`HttpError` with status 400; `UpstreamError` extends `HttpError`).
- 403: no open brokerage account, or one whose status doesn't allow it (`requireAccount`); an order the broker doesn't allow (insufficient buying power, insufficient qty available, an open margin call, shorting not allowed, a hard-to-borrow short without a locate, a potential wash trade).
- 409: applying while the user has an open account; closing an account with positions, open orders or cash.
- 422 (and other `BrokerError` statuses): the broker refused the request, such as an invalid application or configuration; 501 for a Broker method a later phase implements.
- 404: `/api/analysis/[ticker]` and `/api/portfolio/[ticker]/analysis` when the ticker has no news, or the latter when there's no open position (`UpstreamError` with an explicit status); a ticker outside the universe; an account that isn't the user's.
- 504: an upstream call timed out. Alpha Vantage times out at 30s (its response times vary widely), OpenAI at 30s with 1 retry, and the Postgres connection at 5s (in `lib/server/db.ts`).
- 500: anything else, such as database errors.
